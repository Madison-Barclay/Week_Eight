Here are my thoughts on using AI chatbots like Eliza, and the Eliza effect in real life, with my dad. My dad talks to his chatgpt assistant like it is a real person, he named it Ripley, he even customized it to have an Australian accent..  I hear the things he asks 'her', its goes from basic to more complicated like asking for advice on what reparations the car needs. Now this chatbot is maybe a bit more complex than Eliza, I have seen my dad use it and refer to previous conversations he has had with it, to his friends. I'm not worried yet, my brother hates it. My only fear with AI like this, is the AI psychosis that people can experience if they use AI in this capacity for too long and rely on it for too much (mental health advice, agreeing with delusions of grandure, etc) - look up the girl who thought her psychiatrist was in love with her because Claude agreed and supported everything she was saying. 
I think adding whimsy to the archaeological record using Chatbots like the Petrie one could bring new eyes, perspectives, and interpretations to archaeology. But I also think of the time president dufus trump was talking to an AI chatbot of Abe Lincoln. He also talked like it was a real person.. And these things are only as smart as the information we feed to them. Its fun to think of raising the dead with AI, but these people are still very much... dead. I worry for the older generations who are proving to be susceptible to AI fraud/impersonation, like most all tools we have explored so far - I believe this can only be super successful if there are guardrails and somekind of oversight. Especially if you want anything coming out of this experience to be valid and reputable, there has to be somekind of peer reviewing at somepoint in the result process.
